# Prose review

Read the document as its intended reader will encounter it. Judge whether its argument, structure, and wording serve its purpose with the context that reader can reasonably be expected to have. The goal is useful editorial judgment, not a quota of changes.

## Establish the reading context

Identify what the document is meant to explain, enable, record, or persuade the reader to do. Use the request and document to infer the audience, publication setting, and expected prior knowledge. Distinguish a freestanding document from a reply, an instruction from a decision record, and a durable explanation from a progress update. If a missing fact materially changes the review, state the assumption or ask for that fact; otherwise proceed.

Read the whole relevant piece before diagnosing individual sentences. Follow its claims and transitions: what does each part establish, what depends on it, and where does the reader obtain the necessary context? Assess whether the ordering and emphasis match the purpose. A locally accurate sentence can still interrupt the argument or give a minor point disproportionate weight.

## Make qualifications earn their place

For a contrast, caveat, hedge, exception, or aside, consider what work it does here. It may delimit a source's evidence, preserve uncertainty, explain a tradeoff, prevent a likely misconception, or change when a claim applies. Evaluate that function against this audience and purpose, rather than demanding that every qualification be removed or expanded.

Pay particular attention to contrasts introduced by words such as "but", "however", "rather than", and "not". Is the alternative live for this reader, established by the preceding text, or needed to understand the claim? Or does the sentence introduce an unnecessary opponent, defend against an unlikely reading, or answer a conversation the reader never saw? The words themselves are not defects. A qualification can be necessary yet belong closer to the claim it limits, in a narrower form, or in a different part of the document.

Preserve qualifications that change truth, confidence, applicability, or a technical, legal, or safety obligation. Brevity does not justify silently strengthening a claim. Check cited evidence when its scope matters to the editorial judgment; distinguish a wording problem from uncertainty that needs subject-matter verification. Retrieve only relevant, authorized material through read-only sources, and keep private source content within the intended review audience.

## Separate the argument from its drafting history

Notice text that reads as a timeline of generation, successive corrections, or abandoned formulations instead of a coherent account for the reader. Ask whether a detail explains the subject or merely how this particular text came to exist. Necessary evidence and limitations should support the current argument; they do not need a narration of every step that produced it.

Process and history can be substantive. A decision record may need rejected alternatives and the reasons they were rejected; an incident account may depend on chronology; a provenance note may need methods and source limitations. Preserve causal history when it serves that purpose. Where important process information burdens a different document's main argument, suggest a suitable location or companion record conceptually. Do not create one or discard the information by default.

Look for the resulting shape of the whole document: repeated caveats, disconnected corrections, competing purposes, missing premises, and conclusions that arrive before their support. Recommend structural changes where they resolve the problem more cleanly than scattered sentence edits. Preserve coherent passages and the author's voice where they already work.

## Give a private editorial assessment

Lead with the most consequential finding or say that no material change is needed. For each useful note, identify the location, the likely reader effect, why it matters to the document's purpose, and the conceptual change that would address it. A short quote from the existing text can locate a concern. Separate clear problems from optional preferences and facts requiring verification; do not invent objections to make the review look thorough.

Honor explicit user preferences in context. Do not turn every local correction or taste judgment into a universal writing rule. Calibrate the number and depth of notes to the text and the requested review.

Keep the deliverable private editorial analysis. Do not supply replacement passages, polished alternative copy, public-ready review comments, or external actions, and do not offer to produce or post them. Conceptual recommendations can describe moving, consolidating, removing, qualifying, or substantiating a passage without drafting its replacement.

For evaluating or changing this skill, use [behavioral checks](references/behavioral-checks.md). They test judgment, not mandatory output wording.
