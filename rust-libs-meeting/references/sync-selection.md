# Suggest FCPs for synchronous discussion

The output is a shortlist with a decision the room can advance, not a rearranged dashboard.

## Build a current candidate set

Use [agenda and evidence mechanics](agenda-and-evidence.md). Read the latest substantive minutes and any target-meeting announcement for previous decisions, invited participants, carried items, and time already reserved. A generated heading without notes is not prior discussion.

For plausible candidates, establish:

- Canonical proposal and disposition, actual deciding teams, current phase, unchecked reviewers, active concerns and their owners.
- What the current proposal includes and deliberately defers. Follow substantive comments after the proposal; the issue body's old unresolved questions can lag the active stabilization summary.
- What has changed since its last discussion, and what remains unknown. Distinguish unresolved design, missing external input, implementation work, and stale formal state.
- Whose participation could unlock it. Do not assume next week's attendance from last week's list. Check an announced guest or known availability where relevant.

Deduplicate linked polls when they represent one decision, retaining any independently required team decisions. A poll for lang/types/opsem is not a Libs checkbox opportunity merely because it has `T-libs`. It may still merit explicit cross-team coordination if the room has a concrete contribution.

## Rank by what the room can accomplish

Useful candidates tend to fit one of these cases:

| Case | Value of synchronous time |
| --- | --- |
| Bounded decision with live disagreement | Resolve a concrete tradeoff when the needed participants or prepared positions are available. State the alternatives and consequence. |
| New evidence changes a previously blocked decision | Evaluate a Crater result, platform feedback, narrowed proposal, or addressed concern; decide whether it changes the disposition. |
| Substantive agreement, unfinished formal state | Ratify the exact agreed scope and identify who can clear the remaining concern or record review. A concern owner need not be pressured to resolve it. |
| Straightforward proposal awaiting review | A short opportunity for eligible attendees to review the substance. Explain why it appears bounded; "zero concerns" alone is insufficient. |
| Duplicate or stale proposal | Identify the canonical decision and the proposed cancellation/restart/coordination step, with authority and remaining uncertainty. |

Urgency, ecosystem impact, time blocked, review cost, and the time budget modify the order. Few concerns is useful for finding quick items; it does not outrank an urgent decision the room can settle. Do not pad the shortlist to a quota.

Usually leave these asynchronous or defer them with a concrete prerequisite: fresh proposals people need time to read; implementation/rebase-only work; outside-team decisions with nothing for Libs to settle; broad unresolved design without prepared options; an absent stakeholder's unresolved objection when the group cannot address it. Recent items can still be worthwhile when urgent or truly ready for a short decision. An implementation blocker does not prevent useful design ratification, but label that narrower purpose.

An FCP already running or finished is generally not a missing-checkbox item. Bring it back only for a new issue, an explicit remaining decision, or a pending disposition worth coordinating. No meeting can bypass the formal period or another team's approval just by agreeing in the room.

## Give the facilitator something usable

State the target date and when live state was checked. Lead with the best few items in recommended order. For each give the link, a short description, **the question for the room**, why now, current blocker/phase, and the relevant people or preparation if known. A lightweight time estimate is useful when the user gives a time budget.

Group administrative cleanup separately from substantive decisions if that helps the agenda. Briefly explain exclusions that would otherwise look surprising, such as a prominent concern-free but wrong-team poll. Link the exact proposal/concern/resolution evidence for claims that drive the recommendation. If retrieval is partial, identify the missing source and avoid presenting a provisional shortlist as exhaustive.
